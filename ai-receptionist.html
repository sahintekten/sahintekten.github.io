SEDA: an AI receptionist that knows when to stay quiet

What
Answers clinic enquiries on WhatsApp, Instagram and Messenger and books consultations. Pilot with a cosmetic surgery clinic in Istanbul since July 2026.
My role
Co-founder. Agent behaviour, backend, tests, integrations, the pilot itself.
Stack
FastAPI, PostgreSQL, Docker, Retell AI, GoHighLevel, Meta APIs
Results
~300 agent runs a day, ~20 s median reply, 99.2% price accuracy, 267 tests

The problem

Clinics in Turkey's health tourism sector advertise heavily on Instagram and Facebook. Every ad brings a wave of messages, and staff can't always answer them quickly. Slow replies cost clinics enquiries they have already paid for.

An AI that simply answers everything isn't the solution either. It has to quote the right prices, book real appointments, and above all know when not to talk: when a staff member has taken over, when a message arrives on a channel it shouldn't handle, or when it isn't sure.

How it works

We started on a no-code tool, but the flows were fragile and impossible to test, so I moved to a FastAPI backend with one signed entry point.

  1. 1

    A message arrives via Meta into GoHighLevel, the clinic's CRM, inbox and calendar.

  2. 2

    GoHighLevel forwards it to the backend through a single signed webhook.

  3. 3

    The router merges quick bursts into one turn and drops duplicates.

  4. 4

    Gates decide whether the agent may answer at all. If anything is unclear, it stays silent.

  5. 5

    The router adds context, including which of about 80 ad campaign links the person came from.

  6. 6

    The agent on Retell AI runs on a Claude model with the clinic's knowledge base in its prompt; sessions are pinned to a prompt version.

  7. 7

    The agent calls tools: availability, booking and cancelling, notes, sample photos, record lookups.

  8. 8

    The reply goes back through GoHighLevel; bookings sync to Google Calendar and the clinic owner is notified.

Three engineering problems

Knowing when to stay silent

The agent's boundaries are enforced in code, not only in its prompt. Before the agent sees a message, gates check whether a staff member has taken over and whether the channel and page are enabled for the bot. If any check fails or anything is unclear, the system stays silent: it fails closed. A dry-run switch lets us test without sending anything, and after every prompt release we verify the version actually being served.

We test the gates by breaking them. A mutation check script removes each of the 10 safety gates in turn, and the test suite has to fail every time. It does, 10 out of 10. Separately, there are 267 automated tests.

Conversations that outlive a prompt

Sessions are pinned to a prompt version so a conversation doesn't change behaviour midway. The cost: after a pricing update, conversations that started earlier kept quoting old prices and links. I built a session-retirement mechanism with two criteria and retired 2,051 sessions in one go. Each new session starts with a summary of the old one, so nobody had to repeat themselves.

Trusting what comes in and what goes out

Every inbound request is checked against its Ed25519 signature and resolved to a clinic before anything runs; requests that can't be matched are rejected. Messages for one person are processed one at a time, so bursts can't race each other. Every outgoing message is recorded in a ledger, and the ledger is repaired after upstream errors, so the router always knows exactly what it has sent.

The phone agent

Alongside messaging, I built SEDA's phone agent on Retell's Conversation Flow with Make handling the tool calls. It answers calls in Turkish, gives clinic information, checks availability, books and cancels appointments, takes messages and passes urgent cases to the clinic. It has been validated end to end with real test calls but isn't in production yet: the clinic's main line hasn't been routed to it, and we prioritised messaging for the pilot.

Safeguards: cancelling requires looking up the booking first, ownership is checked against the caller's number, requests without a clinic ID are rejected, and the agent never confirms an action until the tool reports success. Validation: real test calls (booking on 4 July 2026, cancellation on 6 July), an 11-scenario notification matrix, a 9-scenario Turkish simulation set, and a daily reconciliation check. To show it in English, I also set up a fictional Dublin clinic on an Irish phone line.

My role

I'm the product owner. I built the FastAPI backend and designed how the agent behaves: its rules and prompts, the knowledge base and pricing, the gates, and the test and release discipline. I set up the GoHighLevel, Meta and WhatsApp Business integrations, run the relationship with the clinic, and lead our production fixes.

What's next
